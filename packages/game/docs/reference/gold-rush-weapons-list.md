# Victorian Gold Rush Weapons List (1850s-1860s)

## Military & Law Enforcement Firearms

### Rifles & Muskets

- **Pattern 1853 Enfield Rifle-Musket** - .577 caliber, 400m range, standard military issue
- **Pattern 1839 Musket** - .753 caliber, smoothbore, used at Eureka Stockade
- **Pattern 1840 Constabulary Carbine** - Police-specific shorter rifle
- **Cavalry Carbines** - Various models, shorter for mounted use

### Revolvers

- **Adams Revolver** - .442 caliber, 5-shot, double-action, British police issue
- **Kerr Revolver** - .442 caliber, 5-shot, police sidearm
- **Colt 1851 Navy** - .36 caliber, 6-shot, adopted by Victoria Police 1864
- **Officer's Adams Double-Action** - Larger stopping power, officer preference

## Civilian Firearms

### Common Miner Weapons

- **Belgian Miner's Pistol** - 22mm caliber, percussion cap, cheap and common
- **Birmingham Pepperbox** - .32-.410 caliber, 4-6 rotating barrels
- **Pocket Pistol** - Small concealed carry, various calibers
- **American Colt Revolver** - Brought by California forty-niners
- **Fowling Piece** - Hunting shotgun, smoothbore

### Bladed Weapons

- **Bowie Knife** - 10-15 inch blade, American import
- **Folding Pocket Knife** - Universal tool and weapon
- **Skinning Knife** - 4-6 inch fixed blade
- **Shillelagh** - Irish fighting stick/club

## Mining Tools as Weapons

### Striking Tools

- **Pickaxe** - 3-7 pound head, pointed and bladed ends
- **Pick Mattock** - Horizontal adze blade, better balanced
- **Cutter Mattock** - Vertical axe blade variant
- **Mining Hammer** - 3-8 pounds, standard rock breaking
- **Mining Sledgehammer** - Up to 25 pounds, 36-inch handle

### Digging & Prying Tools

- **Long-Handle Shovel** - 4-5 feet, reach advantage
- **Short-Handle Spade** - 2.5-3 feet, 4-6 pounds, close combat
- **Crowbar** - 3-5 feet, 8-15 pounds solid iron
- **Pry Bar** - Shorter crowbar variant
- **Wedge and Chisel** - Rock splitting tools

### Cutting Tools

- **Felling Axe** - 3-5 pounds, tree clearing
- **Hand Hatchet** - 1.5-2.5 pounds, utility tool
- **Broadaxe** - Wide blade for timber shaping

### Specialty Mining Equipment

- **Gold Pan** - Heavy iron or tin, improvised shield/weapon
- **Rocker Box Parts** - Wooden components as clubs
- **Sluice Box Rails** - Long wooden beams

## Aboriginal Traditional Weapons

### Projectile Weapons

- **Hunting Spear** - 2.5-3 meters, Burgan tree wood
- **Fighting Spear** - Barbed tips, ceremonial markings
- **Woomera** - Spear-thrower, quadruples force
- **Returning Boomerang** - Heated and twisted for flight
- **Non-Returning Boomerang** - Heavy hunting/fighting variant

### Melee Weapons

- **Mount William Greenstone Axe** - Diabase stone, extremely valuable
- **Nulla Nulla** - 40cm-2m club, ironbark or red gum
- **Leangle** - Victorian-specific angled club
- **Fighting Stick** - Straight hardwood staff
- **Parrying Shield** - 40-120cm mulga wood, carved clan marks

## Makeshift & Improvised Weapons

### Camp Equipment

- **Tent Peg** - Wooden or iron stake
- **Tent Pole** - Wooden staff weapon
- **Mining Chain** - Used as flail
- **Rock in Sock** - Improvised blackjack
- **Broken Bottle** - Jagged glass weapon
- **Lantern** - Thrown as incendiary

### Agricultural Tools

- **Scythe** - Long curved blade
- **Pitchfork** - Multi-pronged, documented at Eureka
- **Pruning Hook** - Curved blade on pole
- **Hay Fork** - Two-pronged variant
- **Reaping Hook** - Small hand scythe

### Blacksmith Tools

- **Blacksmith Hammer** - Various weights
- **Blacksmith Tongs** - Long-handled gripper
- **Horseshoe** - Projectile or knuckle weapon
- **Hot Iron Rod** - From forge
- **Anvil Tool** - Various shaping implements

### Kitchen & Household Items

- **Meat Cleaver** - Favored by Chinese miners
- **Cast Iron Pan** - Shield and bludgeon
- **Fire Poker** - Thrusting weapon
- **Rolling Pin** - Women's defense tool
- **Scissors/Shears** - Stabbing implement
- **Hatpin** - Concealed women's weapon

## Specialized & Unique Weapons

### Bushranger Equipment

- **Stolen Military Carbine** - Various models
- **Horse Pistol** - Large caliber cavalry weapon
- **Stockwhip** - Leather whip, 6-12 feet

### Merchant Protection

- **Coach Gun** - Short double-barrel shotgun
- **Store Revolver** - Behind-counter defense
- **Cash Box** - Reinforced, used as club
- **Guard Dog** - Living weapon

### Chinese Miner Tools

- **Dao** - Chinese broadsword (rare)
- **Agricultural Flail** - Grain threshing tool
- **Carrying Pole** - Bamboo or wood staff
- **Work Cleaver** - Heavy kitchen blade

## Psychological & Torture Weapons (Bushranger Methods)

- **Bull Ants in Boots** - Documented torture device
- **Rope Noose** - Intimidation tool
- **Branding Iron** - Marking victims

## Natural & Environmental Weapons

- **Thrown Rock** - Abundant ammunition
- **Burning Branch** - From campfire
- **Snake in Bag** - Thrown as distraction
- **Mining Explosive** - Black powder charges

---

*Note: This list represents historically documented weapons and tools from the Victorian goldfields 1850s-1860s. Weapon effectiveness, rarity, and social associations varied greatly across different groups and time periods within the gold rush era.*
