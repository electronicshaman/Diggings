# Victorian Gold Rush Remedies & Consumables List (1850s-1860s)

## Patent Medicines & Tonics

### Popular Proprietary Medicines

- **Laudanum** - Opium tincture in alcohol (10% opium), universal painkiller
- **Dr. Kilmer's Swamp Root** - Mysterious herbal tonic claiming to cure all
- **Ayer's Cherry Pectoral** - Alcohol and opium cough syrup
- **Paine's Celery Compound** - 19.85% alcohol "nerve tonic"
- **Chlorodyne** - Opium mixture for coughs and colds
- **Camphorated Tincture of Opium** - For asthma and breathing issues
- **Florida Water** - Perfumed alcohol tonic
- **Dover's Powder** - Opium and ipecac mixture
- **Godfrey's Cordial** - Opium syrup for infants
- **Mrs. Winslow's Soothing Syrup** - Morphine for teething babies
- **Snake Oil Liniment** - Various formulations for joint pain
- **Blow My Skull Off** - Illegal sly grog cocktail with opium, cayenne, rum

### Apothecary Preparations

- **Mercury Compounds** - For syphilis and skin conditions
- **Arsenic Solutions** - For anemia and fevers
- **Belladonna Extract** - For colic and spasms
- **Nitroglycerin** - For headaches
- **Turpentine** - Internal and external use
- **Camphor Oil** - Chest rubs and inhalants
- **Castor Oil** - Laxative
- **Rhubarb Extract** - Digestive aid
- **Ipecac Syrup** - To induce vomiting
- **Calomel** - Mercury chloride purgative
- **Blue Mass Pills** - Mercury pills
- **Paregoric** - Camphorated opium tincture

## Chinese Traditional Medicines (Goldfields)

### Herbal Remedies

- **Ginseng Root** - General tonic and vitality
- **Chinese Tea** - Various medicinal blends
- **Licorice Root** - Cough and throat remedy
- **Chrysanthemum Tea** - Cooling and fever reduction
- **Dried Seahorse** - Ground for various ailments
- **Tiger Balm Precursors** - Herbal ointments
- **Ginger Root** - Digestive aid
- **Star Anise** - Respiratory ailments
- **Chinese Rhubarb** - Stronger than European variety

### Opium Products

- **Raw Opium** - Smoked in dens or eaten
- **Opium Pills** - Coated in varnish, silver, or gold
- **Opium Pipes** - Carved bamboo or ceramic
- **Opium Tins** - Decorated metal containers

## Aboriginal Bush Medicines

### Plant-Based Remedies

- **Tea Tree (Melaleuca)** - Crushed leaves for wounds
- **Eucalyptus Leaves** - Steam inhalation, antiseptic
- **Wattle Bark** - Sleep aid and rheumatism
- **Kangaroo Apple** - Ripe fruit only, green is poisonous
- **Emu Bush** - Antibiotic properties for wounds
- **Native Mint** - Coughs and colds
- **Paperbark** - Bandages and poultices
- **River Red Gum Kino** - Astringent for diarrhea
- **Sandpaper Fig Leaves** - Itch relief
- **Bush Tomato** - Vitamin C source

### Animal-Based Remedies

- **Goanna Oil** - Rubbed on joints
- **Witchetty Grub Fat** - Burns and earache
- **Emu Oil** - Skin conditions
- **Snake Fat** - Rheumatism

## Food & Provisions

### Basic Rations

- **Damper** - Flour, water, salt campfire bread
- **Mutton** - Old sheep, main protein source
- **Salt Beef/Corned Beef** - Preserved meat
- **Flour** - Basic staple, expensive on goldfields
- **Tea** - Black tea, essential beverage
- **Sugar** - Expensive luxury item
- **Salt** - For preservation and seasoning
- **Rice** - Dried import, Chinese staple
- **Lentils** - Dried legumes

### Preserved & Canned Goods

- **Tinned Oysters** - Luxury item
- **Tinned Sardines** - Protein source
- **Tinned Lobster** - Expensive delicacy
- **Pickled Vegetables** - In jars
- **Jam & Preserves** - Fruit preservation
- **Condensed Milk** - Long-life dairy
- **Dried Eggs** - Powdered form
- **Ship's Biscuit/Hardtack** - Long-lasting bread

### Fresh Foods (When Available)

- **Potatoes** - Root vegetable staple
- **Onions** - Flavoring and scurvy prevention
- **Cabbage** - Rare vegetable treat
- **Carrots** - Occasional fresh vegetable
- **Native Berries** - Wild raspberry, geebung fruit
- **Bush Tucker** - Kangaroo, possum, emu
- **Fresh Fish** - Murray cod, trout
- **Wild Duck** - Australian bustard "wild turkey"

### Chinese Market Garden Produce

- **Bok Choy** - Chinese cabbage
- **Bean Sprouts** - Fresh vegetables
- **Spring Onions** - Flavor enhancement
- **Chinese Melons** - Winter melon
- **Ginger** - Fresh root

## Alcohol & Recreational Substances

### Legal Alcohol

- **Rum** - Standard spirit ration
- **Brandy** - Often adulterated
- **Whiskey** - Irish and Scotch
- **Port Wine** - Fortified wine
- **Beer** - When transport allowed
- **Gin** - "Mother's Ruin"

### Sly Grog (Illegal)

- **Blow My Skull Off** - Opium-laced spirits
- **Sly Grog Brandy** - With tobacco and pharmaceutical spirits
- **Pine Beer** - Homemade fermented drink
- **Colonial Wine** - Local grape attempts

### Tobacco Products

- **Plug Tobacco** - Chewing tobacco
- **Pipe Tobacco** - Various cuts
- **Cigars** - Luxury item
- **Snuff** - Powdered tobacco
- **Chinese Tobacco** - Different curing methods

## Health Tonics & Beverages

### Non-Alcoholic Drinks

- **Sarsaparilla** - Root beer-like tonic
- **Ginger Beer** - Fermented ginger drink
- **Lemon Barley Water** - Cooling drink
- **Beef Tea** - Invalid food/drink
- **Fizzy Drinks** - From apothecaries

### Invalid Foods

- **Arrowroot** - Easily digestible starch
- **Sago Pudding** - Tapioca-like food
- **Gruel** - Thin porridge for the sick
- **Chicken Broth** - Restorative soup
- **Wine Jelly** - Invalid dessert

## Hygiene & Personal Care

### Soap & Cleaning

- **Carbolic Soap** - Antiseptic properties
- **Lye Soap** - Homemade from ash
- **Tooth Powder** - Chalk and salt mixture
- **Hair Tonic** - Various alcohol-based formulas

### Skin Preparations

- **Cold Cream** - Face moisturizer
- **Almond Oil** - Skin softener
- **Glycerin** - For chapped hands
- **Witch Hazel** - Astringent
- **Bay Rum** - Aftershave and cologne

## Emergency & Field Medicine

### Wound Treatment

- **Turpentine** - Antiseptic
- **Honey** - Natural antibacterial
- **Spider Webs** - Blood clotting
- **Charcoal Powder** - Poison antidote
- **Vinegar** - Disinfectant

### Pain Relief

- **Willow Bark Tea** - Natural aspirin
- **Clove Oil** - Toothache remedy
- **Hot Mustard Plaster** - Chest congestion
- **Cold Water Treatment** - Hydrotherapy

## Controversial & Dangerous Remedies

### Toxic "Medicines"

- **Lead-Based Compounds** - Face powder, medicines
- **Strychnine** - Small doses as stimulant
- **Cocaine Drops** - Toothache and energy
- **Morphine Injections** - With new syringes
- **Chloroform** - Anesthetic and recreational
- **Ether** - Anesthetic frolic

### Quack Cures

- **Electric Belts** - Magnetism cure-all
- **Radiated Water** - Early radioactive tonics
- **Tapeworm Pills** - Dangerous parasites
- **Blood Letting Tools** - Leeches and lancets

---

*Note: Many of these substances were dangerous, addictive, or ineffective by modern standards. This list represents historical remedies used during the Victorian goldfields era (1850s-1860s) and should not be considered medical advice. The multicultural nature of the goldfields created a unique convergence of European, Chinese, and Aboriginal medical traditions.*
